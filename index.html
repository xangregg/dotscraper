    dotscraper

    Finds the dots in an image of a scatterplot or dot plot and reports where they are, in pixels and, once the axes have values, in data units. It is made for the hard case: dots that overlap, drawn with transparency, where counting blobs or circles fails.

    It works by analysis by synthesis. It measures the dots' style (color, size, transparency, edge blur, outline), sets aside what isn't dots (text, lines, axes), then searches for the set of dot positions whose rendering reproduces the image. A dot hidden in a stack shows up as a darker level, not as an edge.

    Using it

    1. Open an image: Open…, or paste (⌘V / Ctrl+V), or drop one on the view. A PNG straight from the source works best; screenshots and JPEGs work with Noisy color matching.
    2. Check the dot families it found, one per dot color. Add a missing one with Add family by color and a click on a dot; type a radius if it asks for one.
    3. Find dots. The fit runs in the background; Stop keeps what it has.
    4. Review. Hover a dot for its position and uncertainty. Flagged dots are red: hover for why. A magenta double ring marks dots of one family almost in one place, which for opaque dots may be one dot counted twice; the Close pairs box hides these marks. The Residual view shows what the dots don't explain.
    5. Fix with Add, Remove and Move. Edits refit the dots nearby, but never dots you placed or moved. Option-click (Alt-click) adds or removes without any refit.
    6. Axes: tick values are read from the labels when at least three of an axis's fit one scale, or two on a log axis whose minor ticks confirm them; check them, and type over any that is wrong. Labels like 103, 2 × 103 and 1e+05 are read; turned text is not. Otherwise type two or more to get data units.
    7. Export: Copy table (pastes into a spreadsheet) or Save CSV.

    Keys and mouse

    A R M
    Add, Remove, Move mode; Esc ends it
    Arrows
    move the chosen or last added dot 1 px; with Shift, 0.1 px
    D
    show or hide the dots' marks; in Add, Remove and Move, holding Shift hides them
    ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z
    undo an edit
    Wheel, drag
    zoom at the pointer, pan

    What to trust

    • Opaque dots hide one another: a dot entirely behind others can't be found, and a stack's count is a lower bound. Translucent dots show stacks as darker levels, so their counts are better determined.
    • The ± values are standard errors from pixel noise alone. The real error, from resampling and from how well the model matches the drawing, is a few tenths of a pixel.
    • Data values are only as good as the tick values, read or typed, and the ticks it finds. The Axes section gives each axis's resolution in data units per pixel.

    Privacy

    Everything runs in your browser. Images are never uploaded; nothing leaves your computer.
